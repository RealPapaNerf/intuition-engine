# Intuition Engine — decision, 2026-10-22 (later the same day): the lens

The negotiators pack opens, with a coach in the eye line. This refines
the plan of record; where it conflicts with the negotiators brief, this
wins. It does **not** reorder the engineers pack (decisions 2026-10-22
§8 stand: step 3, the import portal, is on its own branch). The two
packs now run in parallel, and the engine's shared parts — the journal,
the predict gate, the coach rule — are what make that cheap.

## 1. The prompt: glasses that help you while you talk

Even Realities' G2 glasses carry a feature they call Conversate: the
glasses listen to the live conversation and put context on the lens
without being asked — terminology explained, a name you didn't catch,
suggested follow-up questions — plus **Prep Notes** you type before a
conversation that stay in view during it, and a summary afterwards.
No camera, a small monochrome display, text in your line of sight.

What they got right, and what we want for negotiation practice: the
hard moment is live, in the room, with eye contact to keep. Help that
arrives afterwards is a debrief; help that arrives during has to be
small, quiet, and already in the eye line. The MBA negotiation
simulations the author is practicing for (two confidential role
sheets, a point schedule per issue, a reservation value) are exactly
the kind of conversation this is built for.

## 2. The inversion: the lens only asks

The glasses' assistant **answers** while you talk. Ours cannot, by
invariant 5: the trustworthy AI never answers, only questions. So the
live layer in this engine — the lens — is the coach, and three things
follow:

1. **The only answers pinned in the lens are your own.** Prep Notes
   become predictions: before the first word you commit to what the
   other side cares about most, where their wall is on the headline
   issue (a band), and what they are actually worried about. They stay
   pinned through the conversation and are scored at the reveal into
   the same journal as the bridge, so calibration is one stat across
   packs.
2. **Cues are questions keyed to public facts.** The lens sees which
   move you made and how they reacted, nothing else; it is handed no
   brief, ever (`negotiation/lens.js` imports nothing, and
   `test/lens.test.js` checks that, that every cue is a question, and
   that none carries a number or a word of the hidden brief).
3. **The trust meter is never shown live.** The glasses cannot read
   minds either. The point is to *predict* the reaction, not watch it;
   the meter appears in the replay with the line where it turned (the
   brief's social slow-motion break).

Where the glasses' "on-the-spot answers" do belong is the Consultant:
labeled, fallible at the calibrated rate, its answers logged as a
separate trust commitment (decisions 2026-10-19 §4). Not built yet.
Its error taxonomy for this pack, for when it is: contradicts something
the other side already said; mistakes a position for an interest;
assumes a fixed pie where the schedule has a compatible issue; gets a
money figure an order of magnitude off; recommends a lever (scarcity,
authority) as a move — the one the kid must learn to refuse.

## 3. Honest ground truth: the MBA simulation's own format

Negotiation's "physics" is the hidden brief, and the MBA simulation
format already is one: issues, options per issue, a point value for
every option for every party, a reservation value, and sometimes a cap
("you cannot approve above $110k"). Every judgement about a deal is
then arithmetic with no judge in the loop: what each side scored,
whether both cleared their walk-away, whether the deal is on the Pareto
frontier, and the money left on the table (a deal better for one and
no worse for the other). `negotiation/brief.js` is that format, frozen
on validation like the materials in `engine/`.

The trust meter moves by fixed rules (`negotiation/trust.js`,
`RULES`, every number `REVISIT`). A mirror draws out a position on the
live topic; a calibrated question draws out the next priority; the
right label of the hidden worry gets "that's right" and a big step;
talking about yourself closes them; an offer far under their line costs
trust unless the accusation audit went first; trust buys flexibility
(the margin over reservation they want shrinks as trust rises); past
each threshold they volunteer more. Discoverable, never arbitrary: the
replay shows every step and why.

## 4. The counterpart: scripted first, as the brief says

`negotiation/counterpart.js` is the state machine: it weighs offers
with its own schedule and counters by asking on the issue with the
most still to gain for it — so a counter reads as a tell about what it
values — and gives information away by the rules above. Pure and
clockless (the feed line holds here too: a test replays the same moves
with and without hours between them and gets the same conversation).

An AI-played character comes later, inside the authored brief, as the
brief planned: the model improvises lines and classifies free-text
moves; the schedule, the reservation, the cap and the trust rules never
move to the model. The zero-dependency rule holds: any model call lives
behind an interface in the demo, never in `negotiation/`.

## 5. The guardrail is code

The brief's hard rule — this track must never become manipulation
training — is a rule of the counterpart, not a note: a pressure move
(a deadline that isn't real) works exactly once, then poisons the
meter on every later use and carries into the next session as the
only thing remembered about you. Levers are scored only as recognition.

## 6. Adult depth is the top of the spiral

The author is Stage 0 for this pack, practicing for MBA simulations.
That is the "model it" rung extended one more turn, not a different
product: the same primitives at K–2 are the orange (one peel, one
juice). The first brief, The Offer Letter, is original and has the
three shapes every schedule teaches: a distributive issue, a logroll,
and a compatible issue that a negotiator who assumes every issue is a
fight will trade away for nothing.

## 7. Hardware is a display target, not a dependency

Even's developer platform (Even Hub) runs third-party apps as web
pages: the phone app loads them in a WebView and relays a text canvas
to the glasses over Bluetooth, with a desktop simulator. The lens is
therefore built as a web page first, in the display's proportions, and
pointing it at real glasses later is packaging, not redesign. Nothing
in the engine depends on the hardware existing.

A real-counterpart mode (an actual classmate across the table) follows
the same shape: prep notes pinned, cues from the moves *you* tag, and
the honest reveal is the two role sheets afterwards. Later.

## 8. What is built, and the next steps

Built: the brief format and Pareto arithmetic, the trust rules, the
scripted counterpart, the predict gate, the question-only lens, The
Offer Letter, and the playable at `demo/lens.html`, with
hand-calculation tests throughout.

Next, in order: a second and third brief (a multi-party one needs the
format to grow); the Consultant's tone-deaf drafts with the taxonomy in
§2; free-text input with model-classified moves behind an interface;
the human-counterpart mode; Even Hub packaging.
